## Findings

Only one of three resources was readable, but it's a substantive, on-topic source: a reference
cataloguing the whole "forward deployed" job family that's grown out of the original Palantir
FDE role.

**The role family, by adoption level**: **Established** roles — Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE),
Software Engineer (FDSE), Creative (FDC), Architect (FDA), Product Manager (FDPM). **Emerging** —
AI Engineer, Designer, Creative Strategist, Product Designer. **Tracked** (early/experimental) —
Marketer, Integration Engineer, Manager, Researcher, and others. This gives "FDE tiers" a second,
complementary meaning beyond seniority levels within the FDE role itself: it's also a family of
sibling roles at different stages of adoption, all sharing one operating model.

**What's actually common across every variant**, stated directly: "customer-embedded, end-to-end
ownership of the outcome, and a working loop between the field and the roadmap" — i.e. the same
three properties `introduction.md`, `the-delta.md`, and `roles-responsibilities.md` already
converged on independently, just restated here as the defining invariant across the entire role
family rather than one specific job title.

**Compensation signal**: engineer and architect variants command the highest base pay (cited up
to ~$390K), with creative-branch roles currently published lower — a rough proxy for how mature
and commercially load-bearing each branch of the family is.

**Pattern of spread**: Palantir's original FDE is explicitly the prototype; as generative AI
expanded into creative, design, and architecture work, companies replicated the *same* embedded
operating model into those domains rather than defaulting to traditional consulting or a
hand-off delivery model — this is the clearest statement so far in this category of *why* the
model keeps getting reused rather than reinvented per-domain.

**Gap to flag**: this topic's title promises "tiers" (implying seniority levels: junior/senior/
staff FDE, or similar), but the one readable source gives role-family breadth instead of
within-role seniority levels. The two Forbes/towardsdatascience sources that 403'd might have
covered the seniority-tier angle more directly — worth retrying if either domain ever becomes
fetchable, though not escalating to `fde:needs-better-sources` since a real, substantive, on-topic
finding was recorded.
