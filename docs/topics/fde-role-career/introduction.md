## Findings

**Definition** (rocketlane.com): an FDE "implements, tailors, troubleshoots, and operationalizes
your product *from inside the customer's reality*" — a customer-embedded engineer making complex
software actually work in a real, messy deployment environment, not a consultant producing
recommendations from the outside.

**Origin**: the role traces to Palantir in the early 2010s, arising from a practical gap — not a
trend — between what a product could do and the messiness of actual enterprise environments.
Customers needed engineers embedded on their own teams, not advisors. The model later spread to
Intercom, Stripe, and Datadog as products (especially AI-driven ones) grew more complex.

**Five core responsibilities**: accelerate complex technical implementations (unblock
integrations/data issues); operationalize outcomes so the customer actually hits its ROI timeline
(direct link to `roi-ai-impact.md`'s findings); bridge the gap between go-to-market and
engineering without derailing the product roadmap; surface hidden workflow problems before they
become churn; feed field learnings back to the product team.

**Essential skills, beyond engineering ability**: fast problem identification in messy
environments; customer empathy paired with clear communication; "founder-like ownership" of
outcomes; comfort with ambiguity and fast iteration; product judgment (telling a real
customization need apart from a genuine product-roadmap gap); AI fluency specifically for
designing and deploying agentic systems.

**Key differentiator from adjacent roles** (Customer Success Engineer, Solutions Architect,
Implementation Consultant): an FDE operates *inside* the product, extending it to the customer's
actual needs — not working around the product's limits from outside it.
