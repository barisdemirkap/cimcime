## Findings

**What it is, and the key terminology distinction** (simplystakeholders.com): identifying,
understanding, and building relationships with people/organizations that affect or are affected
by the work. **Management** is the broader systematic process; **engagement** is the specific
interactions within it (meetings, consultations, communications) — management is the strategy,
engagement is its execution.

**Core techniques**: identification + analysis (needs, interests, influence, how each
stakeholder is actually affected); **mapping** via named frameworks — the Power/Interest Grid or
the Salience Model — to prioritize who matters most and why; relationship mapping *between*
stakeholders (who influences whom, where the relationship gaps are — not just a flat list);
documented engagement plans (approach, responsibilities, success measures per stakeholder);
continuous monitoring (log interactions/issues/commitments, track sentiment changes over time).

**Why it matters** (boreal-is.com): stronger relationships, proactive risk mitigation,
better-informed decisions via stakeholder insight, improved reputation, regulatory/social
alignment. Process: identify (internal: employees/managers; external: regulators, communities,
suppliers) → analyze (interests/influence/potential conflicts) → build a documented engagement
plan → apply best practices (start early, communicate honestly, listen actively — not just
inform).

**A notable, specific technology point** (boreal-is.com): Stakeholder Relationship Management
(SRM) tooling is framed as distinct from CRM — purpose-built for long-term, cyclical
relationships rather than a linear sales funnel. Worth knowing as vocabulary if a client asks
about "stakeholder tooling" and means something different from their sales CRM.

**AI's role, named explicitly** (simplystakeholders.com): AI increasingly helps analyze
engagement data at scale to surface patterns/emerging issues a manual review would miss — but
human judgment stays essential for *interpreting* those patterns and deciding what to do, a
direct instance of this project's own "AI owns how, human owns why" rule (ADR-0001) stated from
a completely unrelated source.

**Direct relevance to this system**: the "Power/Interest Grid" concept maps loosely onto how
this whole project has been sequenced — categories and approval gates were prioritized based on
what the user (the one real stakeholder) cared about most (e.g. jumping to "continue learning"
over finishing ticket #6 when the Telegram token wasn't ready), not strictly the originally
planned ticket order.
