### NN/g: Usability Testing 101
- Definition: an observational UX-research method. A facilitator has a participant perform tasks on one or more interfaces, watches what they do, and listens for feedback.
- Three components: facilitator (guides, avoids influencing behavior), tasks (realistic; wording matters since phrasing can prime or confuse), participant (a realistic user, often thinking aloud).
- Purpose: find design problems, uncover opportunities, learn user behavior and preferences. Even good designers need real-user testing because there are too many variables. It supports iterative design.
- Qualitative testing (finds problems, more common) vs quantitative (task success, time on task; for benchmarks).
- Remote moderated resembles in-person; remote unmoderated uses an online tool and participants work alone.
- Cost scales up with competitive/international testing, multiple user groups, quantitative studies, eye-trackers, labs, detailed reports.
- Cheap study: about three days. Plan, test five users, analyze and turn findings into redesign recommendations. Five participants is the recommended size for a typical qualitative study of one user group.
- No formal step-by-step process is given in the article.

### Link between the two
Prototypes (e.g. built in Figma) are the artifact you put in front of participants in a usability test before building the real thing. This link is my synthesis; neither source states it directly.
