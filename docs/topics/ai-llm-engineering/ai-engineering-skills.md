## Findings

**What AI engineers do** (coursera.org): build AI/ML-powered applications and infrastructure,
conduct statistical analysis, build models, and convert them into usable APIs — framed around
business outcomes (efficiency, cost, profit, decisions), not research for its own sake.

**Core skill stack**: programming (Python, R, Java, C++); math (probability, statistics, linear
algebra); big-data tooling (Spark, Hadoop, MongoDB — directly connecting back to `spark.md` in
the data-engineering category); ML/AI frameworks (TensorFlow, PyTorch, Keras).

**Career context**: median US salary ~$138K, projected growth 20% from 2024-2034 (well above
average occupation growth). Entry path doesn't strictly require a CS degree — professional
certificates and a demonstrable project portfolio are accepted alternatives, though many
employers still prefer a CS/IT bachelor's.

**Gap worth flagging**: this is a generic "AI Engineer" skills overview, not specific to the
FDE variant of that skill set. The one video whose title directly promised to compare "AI
Engineering vs. AI Forward Deployed Engineer" was unfetchable — this is exactly the kind of
clarification this topic needs and didn't get. Combined with `fde-tiers.md`'s own unresolved
question (role-family breadth vs. seniority tiers), this suggests the AI-Engineer-vs-FDE
distinction specifically is a recurring gap across this roadmap's source set.
