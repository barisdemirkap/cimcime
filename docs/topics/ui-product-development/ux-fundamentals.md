## Findings

### Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics (NN/g)
1. Visibility of system status: keep users informed with timely feedback (analogy: "You Are Here" map markers).
2. Match between system and real world: use users' language, not internal jargon; natural, logical order (analogy: stovetop knobs laid out like burners).
3. User control and freedom: clearly marked "emergency exit"; support undo, redo, cancel.
4. Consistency and standards: same words/actions mean the same thing; follow platform and industry conventions.
5. Error prevention: remove error-prone conditions or confirm before commit. Slips (inattention) and mistakes (wrong mental model) need different prevention.
6. Recognition rather than recall: make options visible, don't make users remember across screens; contextual help.
7. Flexibility and efficiency of use: accelerators hidden from novices, speed experts; personalization/customization.
8. Aesthetic and minimalist design: drop irrelevant or rarely needed information; each extra element competes with what matters.
9. Help users recognize, diagnose, recover from errors: plain language, no codes, state the problem precisely, suggest a fix, clear visual treatment.
10. Help and documentation: ideally unneeded; otherwise searchable, task-focused, concise, in context, with concrete steps.

### Laws of UX (lawsofux.com index, 30 items)
Grouped by my own reading, from the one-line definitions only:
- Perception/grouping (Gestalt): Common Region, Proximity, Prägnanz, Similarity, Uniform Connectedness.
- Cognition/memory: Cognitive Load, Chunking, Miller's Law (about 7 plus or minus 2 items in working memory), Working Memory, Selective Attention, Serial Position Effect, Von Restorff Effect, Zeigarnik Effect, Peak-End Rule, Cognitive Bias, Mental Model.
- Decisions/performance: Hick's Law (decision time grows with choices), Choice Overload, Fitts's Law (farther and smaller targets take longer), Doherty Threshold (respond within about 400ms), Goal-Gradient Effect, Flow.
- Design philosophy: Jakob's Law (users expect your site to work like others), Postel's Law (accept liberally, send conservatively), Tesler's Law (complexity can only be moved, not removed), Occam's Razor, Pareto Principle, Parkinson's Law, Paradox of the Active User (people skip manuals), Aesthetic-Usability Effect.

### Connections
- Jakob's Law ties to heuristic 4 (consistency); Mental Model ties to heuristic 2; Miller's Law and Cognitive Load tie to heuristics 6 and 8; Hick's Law/Choice Overload tie to 8; Paradox of the Active User ties to 10; Doherty Threshold ties to 1.
- Tesler's Law is relevant to FDE work: complexity moved off the user lands on the system or integrator.
